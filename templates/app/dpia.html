DPIA summary

Article 35-style assessment under Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023.

1. Processing description

QuestionAnswer
Nature of processingRecording patient screening, referral, appointment, encounter and disposition events in a hash-chained append-only log
ScopeAdults 30–79 with a hypertension-positive screening in Nigeria
ContextLicensed community pharmacies (primary channel), PMVs under approved protocol (secondary), HMO or hospital as payer
PurposesCare completion, referral tracking, payer audit, program reporting
Data subjectsPatients screened for hypertension
Categories of dataBP reading, symptoms, age band, insurance type, NHIA number (if applicable), phone number, care events
Special category dataNone. BP is health data but not one of the Article 9 special categories under NDPA §30.
RecipientsReferring pharmacy, receiving hospital, payer (HMO / NHIA / donor) — never sold, never shared with third parties
RetentionCare events: 7 years (regulatory). Contact details: crypto-shredded on consent withdrawal, with chain preserved via tombstoned hash.

2. Necessity and proportionality

The minimum data needed to close a care loop is: a BP reading, an outcome, a way to reach the patient, and proof the sequence was not altered. We store all four. We deliberately do not store: full name (only derived initials in receipts), home address, gender, ethnicity, free-text notes, or any clinical narrative. Contact details are hashed for lookup and encrypted for outreach, with separate keys.

4. Data subject rights

  • Access: patient sees own receipt at /api/patient/<id>/receipt with phone OTP (BUILD)
  • Rectification: new corrective event appended; original event preserved (BUILD)
  • Erasure: consent revocation triggers crypto-shredding of PII, chain preserved as hashes-only (BUILD)
  • Portability: JSONL export at /api/audit/export (BUILD)
  • Objection: opt-out recorded as PATIENT_DECLINED event (BUILD)
